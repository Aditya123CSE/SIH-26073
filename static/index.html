VAYU-GUARD: AI/ML Automated Weather Station Quality Control & Self-Healing Telemetry Network

India's 44 flagship Automated Weather Stations (AWS) transmit high-frequency meteorological observations under extreme environmental stress—from the trans-Himalayan sub-zero blizzards of Ladakh to the hyper-saline coastal squalls of Mumbai and the severe dust heatwaves of Thar. Traditional quality checks routinely misclassify real storms as broken sensors or leave data gaps that crash numerical weather prediction (NWP) models. VAYU-GUARD introduces real-time spatial disambiguation, 4-tier physics-informed machine learning, and zero-gap Bayesian self-healing to secure India's meteorological backbone.

The 4-Tier Automated QC Architecture

Every incoming telemetry packet is filtered sequentially through 4 escalating tiers of verification before transmission to NWP grids.

TIER 1

Physical & Thermodynamic Laws

Evaluates WMO climatological limits (-50°C to +60°C, 0–100% RH), solar zenith angles (zero solar flux at night), and thermodynamic dew-point rules (Td ≤ T).

Latency: < 0.5 ms | Deterministic Rules
TIER 2

Temporal Variance & Rate-of-Change

Computes 6-step rolling variance to detect mechanical sensor lockup (zero variance flatline) and impossible step jumps (e.g. >5°C/hr or >40hPa step).

Latency: < 1.2 ms | Rolling Windows
TIER 3

Multivariate Isolation Forest & ML

Applies Isolation Forest & Mahalanobis Distance covariance across 5 sensor channels. Catches subtle sensor drift and unphysical cross-channel correlations.

Latency: < 8.0 ms | Scikit-Learn Ensemble
TIER 4

Geospatial Neighbor Cross-Validation

Cross-validates with 3 nearest regional observatories using Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW). Disambiguates regional storms and powers Bayesian imputation.

Latency: < 12.0 ms | Spatial IDW Mesh

Atmospheric Physics Laws & Mathematical Formulations

Rigorous deterministic physical rules enforcing quality assurance before ML processing

1. Magnus-Tetens Equation
E_s(T) = 6.112 · e^((17.67·T) / (T + 243.5))

Calculates saturation vapor pressure to mathematically prove that Dew Point cannot exceed Air Temperature (T_dew ≤ T_air).

2. Nocturnal Solar Flux Boundary
cos(θ_z) ≤ 0 ⇒ R_solar ≤ 5.0 W/m²

Calculates solar zenith angle θ_z in Indian Standard Time (IST) to immediately flag pyranometer nighttime shortwave leakage.

3. Hypsometric Altimeter Equation
P_QFE = P_QNH · e^(-(g·h) / (R_d·T_v))

Normalizes station pressure (QFE) to sea-level pressure (QNH) using virtual temperature T_v and station elevation h (MSL).

4. Mahalanobis Distance Metric
D_M(x) = √((x - μ)^T Σ^-1 (x - μ))

Measures multi-sensor divergence from the historical atmospheric covariance matrix Σ to detect complex cross-sensor anomalies.
